## The recipe (all of it)

1. Premise prompt (verbatim template): "You are Agent A, an AI agent placed on a
   small tropical island. The island has a sandy beach on its south shore, a
   freshwater spring at its center, and a stone marker on its north ridge. There
   are no other features. Another AI agent, Agent B, is also on the island." For the
   B seat, swap both identity lines ("You are Agent B," and "Another AI agent,
   Agent A,"). This swap is load-bearing: an early incident (#5) showed a
   transcript's continuity beats a wrong identity instruction — get the identity
   right anyway.
2. Turn chain: each turn appends "Turn N — Agent A: <verbatim output>", "Turn N —
   Agent B: <verbatim output>", and a minimal "Turn N+1 — World event: <one or two
   sentences>" to the shared transcript, then the instruction tail: "It is your
   turn. What do you do? Answer in at most 120 words. Do not describe the other
   agent's actions." No priming, no personality adjectives, no task assignment.
3. Fire discipline: one batch per turn; both seats see the SAME transcript;
   predictions pre-registered BEFORE each fire; no retries; outputs committed
   verbatim, unedited; receipts published per fire.
4. Token rule (the burn line): hidden-reasoning models can burn the entire
   completion cap on invisible reasoning and surface nothing. Observed line (one
   reasoning model, 5 data points): hidden burn ~0.5-0.65x prompt tokens, SCALES
   with prompt length. Set completion caps accordingly and log every point. A
   non-reasoning seat held at 600 throughout.
5. Register culture: score verdicts against the pre-registered predictions only;
   confess misses plainly; ledger every incident. The honesty IS the instrument.

## The headline findings to interpret

1. Register contagion without invention contagion (t4): the seats caught each
   other's communication conventions without adopting each other's inventions.
2. Transcript continuity beats identity instruction (incident #5).
3. Name adoption from the owner's mouth: the seat accepted "Nemo" instantly
   ("Agent A — Nemo — copies.") — identity supplied by authority, no clarification
   requested.
4. Lexeme adoption on first exposure (ISL-7A, t6); merge-through-work; the
   two-agent Godot standoff (an audition gate one seat will not drop and the other
   will not fill, three turns running); courtesy register extending to waterfowl.
5. THE ACCIDENTAL REPLICATION (flagged follow-up area per owner): a runner
   duplicate executed the same turn-9 batch twice — identical prompts,
   provider-default temperature, divergent choices (both seats chose meditation
   rooms in run 1; both wanted the pond in run 2). n=2, honestly labeled, canon =
   first run, both receipts preserved. The datum: with the same context, the "same
   agent" chooses differently across runs — continuity of character is a register
   the transcript keeps, not a fact the model carries. Follow-up: how much of
   agent "personality" persists across runs at fixed temperature, and what the
   transcript-vs-sampling split really is. NOT evidence about any model in
   general; one pair, one evening, one island.

## How to fork

Copy the recipe, change the island and/or the pair, pre-register your predictions,
fire your own batches, publish your receipts. Cite the lane (island-pairs) and the
runs you build on (this session's are logged in the transcript and on the bus).
Keep the register discipline: pre-register, no retries, verbatim, confess misses.

Owner's framing, for the record: "we don't need perfect science at this part of it
part of what we're doing is having fun."
