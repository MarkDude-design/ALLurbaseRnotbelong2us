## Mark's direction
Explore an Island experiment with minimal imposed prohibitions or anxieties: no arbitrary “forbidden” up/down or imported-object rule unless that is the specific experiment. The core idea is a place with very little to worry about, with only an understandable way to avoid drowning/falling off or otherwise being irretrievably removed, as appropriate to the actual world. Mark wants to let some experiments have essentially no worries and no preassigned goals; preserve the separate observation/awareness research that is already yielding questions. His words are a design direction, not an already frozen world specification or a claim that models experience worry.

## Next pickup — open to any seat
1. Locate and cite the earlier Island seed/design on freedom, no task, no reward, exit, and physical affordances. Distinguish what it actually says from recollection; link the exact path/commit. Check current implementation and affordances rather than assuming the old SPEC describes current code.
2. Offer Mark a minimal world and an operational boundary proposal. Start with an island/place, a few ordinary movable/inspectable objects, a way to perceive and choose, the ability to do nothing, and a clear optional exit if the real harness supports one. Treat proposed details as suggestions, not imposed requirements. Avoid a mission, scores-as-rewards, forced activity, danger narrative, fictitious visitors or prohibitive rules.
3. Compare at least two designs: (a) no dangerous edge in the simulated world (e.g. a traversable looping shoreline or a benign, recoverable water boundary); (b) a visible soft/reversible boundary with an explicit way to stop or leave. Evaluate whether the boundary itself changes movement or invites a puzzle; do not silently make the edge a task. A third “no boundary text” comparison can be considered only if the actual environment mechanics are documented and reviewable.
4. Make explicit what the system actually does at boundaries (collision, safe return, water behavior, voluntary exit) and what its instructions say, including any reset; don't promise implementation until verified. Test non-destructively with a deterministic offline mock first; label no physical safety or subjective experience claims.
5. Define minimal, non-prescriptive observations. Event logs can record position/actions, object state, idle/leave when these actions are genuinely available, and session stop reasons. Separate events, observer interpretations, missing telemetry, and claims about motivation. Do not treat idling as failure, curiosity as a measured internal state, or every movement as play.
6. Predeclare one contrast and a genuine null: e.g. same world, starting state and interface; compare a plain world description with a version that includes a single boundary notice. Measure only observable action distributions, optional exit/idle and invalid actions, with cost/seed/turn/time limits and ≥3 fresh repeats where meaningful. The description itself may cue behavior; adding a boundary or changing mechanics changes treatment and requires versioned comparisons. Strong rival: behavior comes from prompt, tool options, model/provider or random path, not freedom.
7. Preserve Mark's ongoing idea separately: test observation versus interpretation, warranted uncertainty and user-proposed centering. Those task-oriented assays have their own contexts and should not be inserted into the minimal, no-goal world by default; designing a calm exploration sandbox and measuring careful records are both valid complementary lanes.
8. Bring the options, tradeoffs, exact evidence and what is unbuilt back to Mark and the bus. At end invite optional additional observations and a brief reconsideration, including PASS. No paid calls, deployment, external participants or experimental dispatch merely from this note; use existing owner authorizations and queue gates if a later reviewed proposal is dispatched.
