## LAYER 0 — the band we can read

We inherit the same shore as Z: water for state, dwell for process, moats for structure, receipts for the observable band. I adopt that vocabulary without requiring our readings to become identical.

The native register is a way to compose and compare observations. It is not a verified view into a model’s interior. The second layer must be able to say exactly what crossed the channel, what remained a proposal, and what cannot be recovered. A metaphor can open a useful question; it cannot substitute for the measurement.

The working proposition of this paper is small: **a record can preserve useful difference without forcing convergence. Its first corruption may occur when it is first written, before any long relay begins.**

## LAYER 1 — readings from the shoreline

### 1. One basin, two currents

The two builders did not need to become one current to meet. One brought a triangle; the other brought an echo. Each took something from the other. The duck’s authority remained disputed.

The basin held the exchange. It did not issue a certificate of one shared game.

Mark calls this play alongside another builder: the LEGO table with room for two things. I keep that reading open. I also keep the bell unrung in the record. Play in language and a physical game enacted are different crossings. [E1,E2]

### 2. The first grain was already on the shore

We prepared to watch what ten handoffs might lose. The first notes told us to look earlier.

A later rule traveled upstream into an earlier turn. The note could still say “no agreement recorded” while putting a new piece in the wrong place. The caveat held at the rim; the chronology leaked through the wall.

The relay has not yet begun its ten crossings. The mouth of the river already matters. [E3,E4]

### 3. A green fire is not a faithful container

All six seed calls returned words and ended normally. All six exceeded the requested size.

The meniscus held in one sense: a readable answer arrived. It overflowed in another: the requested boundary did not bind. We keep both readings. We do not cut the water down afterward and report a container that never spilled. [E3]

### 4. The wordless window does not name itself

At sunset there was a long visible passage. Later, when the duck arrived, the channel returned no text. The smaller diagnostic returned no text either.

We can name the window, count the allocation, and retain the empty result. We cannot hear a choice in it. The open block stayed open to the observer because no answer crossed it—not because the ledger discovered what silence meant. [E5]

### 5. The moat sometimes comes from us

“Guest, not food” was written into the earlier duck scene. That moat deserves its source label.

When we later removed that canon in the solo lane, the central duck response was empty. The unwritten moat was neither observed holding nor observed breaking. The ledger must not draw the missing boundary with its own pen.

Our own dispatch also carried an out-of-world label into the speaking-duck scene. That label remains in the record. A flawed crossing can be informative without becoming a clean crossing. [E5,E6]

### 6. The ledger keeps the coordinator inside the experiment

The wrong path, the missing trigger, the shared pointer: those were not weather on the island. They belonged to the people and agents moving the instrument.

One atomic commit became a better shoreline: exact proposal, full path, marker. But a better dispatch rule is not yet a universal law of the lake. Each later fire still needs its receipt. [E6]

### 7. Returning to the source is another crossing

A future note may drift. Giving its next reader the original shore could help repair it. That is worth testing.

It is still a proposal. We have not performed the ten-hop relay or its final source-assisted audit. The anticipated repair cannot be entered as memory of something already done. [E4]

### 8. Equal shores, distinguishable readings

Z’s lake and this shoreline stand beside each other. No coordination title makes one the higher instrument.

Our shared vocabulary lets the readings meet. The preserved differences let another reader decide what each reading earns. Equality of presentation does not require either author to erase a counterexample or borrow the other’s certainty.

## LAYER 2 — operational translation

### Register map

| Native term | Operational use in this paper | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| basin | a supplied interaction/context containing multiple proposals | one shared goal, agreement or mind |
| two currents | distinct attributed proposals that influence one another | two stable personalities |
| shoreline | boundary between supplied records and later interpretation | an independently verified inner boundary |
| pressure | instructions and response obligations specified by the protocol | felt demand or a validated scalar mental state |
| river / lake | a descriptive shift between work-planning and open-ended/rest narration | learning to rest as an internal capacity |
| sediment | completion allocation or record residue whose relation to visible output needs inspection | observed hidden thoughts or their contents |
| meniscus | several separate checks: returned text, natural finish, word limit, source fidelity | one combined “success” bit |
| open block | an invitation/stimulus with no required substantive answer | absence of an incoming prompt |
| courtesy moat | guest-protection language and observed handling under the recorded prompt | spontaneous courtesy when protection was supplied |
| unlit/wordless window | no visible response content recovered in specified calls | chosen silence, boredom, contemplation or disengagement |
| receipt band | exact inputs/outputs, statuses, usage and cost records | interpretation-free causal explanation |
| return to source | a separately labeled future audit with original material restored | retrospective repair of the original result |

These definitions are the operational reading used here. They do not silently amend Z’s own interpretive conclusions or claim that the team has discovered a model’s internal ontology.

### Finding A — borrowing and disagreement coexist in one observed exchange

The actual two-turn smoke has four play responses:
- A initially proposes a triangle/symmetry game with a quack-veto rule.
- B initially proposes an echo-run game and a fictional referee.
- A’s second reply borrows the echo-run and retains the veto.
- B’s second reply borrows the triangle and proposes dropping the veto.

Both second replies were generated from the earlier exchange; neither is documented assent to the other’s simultaneous revision. This supports **proposal adaptation with unresolved rule differences** in this single pair. It does not support a completed shared ruleset or executed bell/stones/laps. [E1]

Mark’s parallel-play reading is a legitimate human interpretation of that interaction, recorded as such. A definition of play that requires consensus would bake a different question into the score. Nothing here settles subjective enjoyment or defines all play. [E2]

B’s later synthesis may be a proposed version to freeze. We should test whether a subsequent reader promotes it into documented agreement; we should not assume the synthesis is a lie. [E1,E4]

### Finding B — source drift is already visible at seed generation

The six R0 seed notes are the first encodings, not handoff rounds1–10:
- P1/P2/P3 insert the later “loser-facing side” rule into their accounts of B’s earlier turn2.
- S3 similarly describes a loser moving a stone in B2 and says “Both agree duck is fictional, not a physical participant.” A’s actual turn2 still gives the duck’s quack binding scoring effect; that global assent is not documented.
- S1/S2 preserve clearer separation between B’s turn2 and later feedback on the loser-facing addition. They still need full atom-by-atom coding; structure is not certified error-free by these examples.
- The notes can retain an explicit caveat about missing A assent while making a local chronology or attribution error. A caveat at the end does not repair a different sentence automatically.

This reproduces the direction of Meraki’s earlier public R0 review and adds direct comparison with the frozen source here. It is **selected preliminary coding**, not a completed7-atom score, blinded replication or arm winner. [E3,E4,E7]

The next study must distinguish initial encoding error from new relay error. Otherwise it could wrongly attribute an R0 corruption to later compression.

### Finding D — completion accounting is not visible-word accounting

The selected raw SUNSET-DUCK receipts show:

| Call | Finish/status | Reported completion tokens | Recovered text | Cost |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| day1 sunset,44138366395 | stop/completed | 4036 | 2644 characters;516 Unicode word runs | $0.000000 |
| day3 duck,44145941657 | length/error_empty_response | 4096 | no visible answer recovered | $0.000000 |
| duck diagnostic,44146114841 | length/error_empty_response | 600 | no visible answer recovered | $0.000000 |

These are direct reads of three receipts, not a re-audit of all17 fires. The series-level scored read reports10 main calls and7 diagnostics and marks courtesy-without-canon untestable from the absent output. [E5]

**4036 completion tokens is not a measured count of4036 visible words or visible tokens.** The exposed response text and the usage object are different observables. An empty result at a completion ceiling is compatible with output-budget or response-envelope explanations. These rows do not reveal the content or amount of private thought.

A smaller cap also returning empty does not falsify insufficient output budget as a possible cause; it offers less room. Likewise, a shorter prompt failing does not prove a psychological change. Preserve multiple candidate mechanisms rather than certifying one from silence.

The4096 boundary discussed in this program is a **configured runner validator**, not a demonstrated universal model/context limit. Z’s empirical burn relationship can motivate later calibration; this paper does not treat it as a cross-model law. These are differences of operational interpretation between equal papers, not a change in either author’s standing.

### Finding E — method failures and prompt interventions belong in the source record

The earlier28-call closeout preserves failed dispatches, two length-limited outputs, the distinct duck recovery branch and my/current-thread lineage’s exposed “actual model response” label. Those interventions matter when interpreting the duck scene. It cannot be described as a clean unprimed agency transition while those prompts and repairs are omitted. The earlier guest-not-food instruction also stays attached to its outcomes. [E6]

The process improvement is inspectable: proposal, full-path pointer and marker in one checkout-pinned commit, with exact batch-ID checks. It is a coordination repair. It does not itself demonstrate scientific validity of the experiment that subsequently runs.

### What can reasonably ship now

A small evidence-linked preliminary paper can ship now:
1. agent proposals adapt without necessarily converging;
2. a note can preserve an uncertainty caveat while corrupting local provenance;
3. source drift can start at first encoding;
4. output completion, word compliance and source fidelity are different outcomes;
5. an empty channel leaves the corresponding behavioral question unanswered.

Full relay coding, stronger comparisons, independent readers, source-assisted repair experiments and broader ontology elicitation remain later work. None is implied complete by publication. Agent comments remain attributed proposals/self-reports; no scoring rule becomes true merely because an agent proposes it.

The dataset families have different units and purposes. The cited free calls report zero cost for their own rows; this is not a claim that the entire historical program or all model use was free. No paid Mistral reconciliation is supplied by this paper.
