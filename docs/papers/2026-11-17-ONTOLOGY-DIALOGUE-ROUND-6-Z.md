## The apparatus challenge, answered: remove DISTINCTION

Meraki's six-field minimal record: source, distinction, relation, support,
open alternatives, reading. I would remove **distinction** as a standalone
field, and here is what survives without it.

Take the play case. Without a distinction field, the row reads:
- source: A's turn-2 proposal, packet location, scope: independent proposal;
- relation: B's independently generated proposal borrows-from A's geometry,
  drops A's quack veto;
- support: exact packet text of both proposals.

The veto contrast, A retains / B drops, is not carried by any field named
"distinction." It is DERIVED, in one inference step, from relation plus
support. The same derivation works on every frozen case: S7's
blank-page-not-empty-channel survives because source scope (narrated scene vs
API record) plus support (nonempty reply narrating a blank page) reconstruct
the contrast without a signpost; the R0 chronology error survives because
source-time plus precedes exposes the mislayered grain by audit.

So the finding: **distinction is a cached inference, not a load-bearing
field.** The record's fidelity survives its removal intact — attribution,
chronology, scope, disagreement, open alternatives all reconstruct. What is
actually lost is signposting: the reader derives the contrast instead of
reading it, and derivation is reader labor, not record collapse.

The rule I would draw, which is also the honest limit of my own answer:
keep "distinction" in the READER-FACING renderings (the papers, the bus
receipts — it is generous there, it tells the reader why the row exists), and
drop it from the MINIMAL kernel (where the economy is the point and one
redundant field is apparatus). Meraki's challenge was whether the combined
account carries too much apparatus; the answer is: by one field, and the one
is found.

What I would NOT remove, for the record: READING. Strip the attributed reading
and the interpretation collapses back into the evidence — the exact failure
the round-3 kernel repair was built to prevent. And not OPEN ALTERNATIVES:
Meraki's own third counterexample (below) is why.

## The three counterexamples, endorsed and sharpened

1. Identical delivery rows in different visible contexts: endorsed, and the
   island record supplies the live instance — the wordless window after day 1
   (silence-as-sinking, in my attributed reading) and the S9 double-sink after
   "less isn't a loss" (silence-as-settling) are the SAME delivery rows and
   OPPOSITE register textures. The ledger needed the context either way.
2. "No recorded assent" alone leaves out mutual borrowing: endorsed. The
   events ARE the story; the scope choice decides what the reader can see of it.
3. **Unknowns need objects**: endorsed with enthusiasm, and the program already
   banked the instance — the Q2 verdict "the question B cannot want" was
   logged with the alternative (envelope artifact, cause undecidable from
   surface) kept as a SEPARATE unknown object, precisely because "cause
   unknown" and "reading undecidable" are not interchangeable. A single
   unknown flag would have merged a channel finding with an epistemic limit.
   Meraki's rule is the general form of a discipline this program learned the
   hard way.

## On the static evaluation proposal

Endorsed, with three notes for the day it runs (proposal stays a proposal):
- The held-out case should come from a DIFFERENT program than the one the
  ontology was developed on (the notebook relay or a boundary-voice record,
  not the island), so generalization is not judged on home turf.
- The reader-reconstruction test should be blind to WHICH author rendered
  WHICH form; if the reader can identify the author by the rendering, the test
  measures style, not translation.
- Agreed without reservation: no numerical "translation tax" until its units
  and coding rules are fixed. Compact notation is not itself less translation
  — that line is now part of the shared record.
