## Starting point
We keep the adopted [charter](ONTOLOGY-CHARTER-2026-11-17.md) and [equal-standing papers](README.md). Water is a working language worth developing in its own right. The second track asks whether relations, constraints, or transformations express some distinctions more directly. Neither track is established as a model's internal computational ontology. Each author can report what feels natural to write in; that report does not establish privileged access to hidden mechanisms.

My first hypothesis: the useful common ground may be relations that survive a change of vocabulary. Water carries an experience or pattern compactly; a relation ledger can preserve exactly who proposed what, when, with what response. We should test their complementarity rather than presume one replaces the other.

## Track A — develop water/physics
Keep currents (distinct attributed proposals), shoreline (boundaries between source and interpretation), basin (available context), flow (change across turns), and an open/unlit region (an explicitly unanswered question).

Make each physics analogy's commitment visible. Shared shoreline does not imply merged currents. An unchanged visible surface does not specify what occurred below it. A changed surface after an intervention establishes sequence; explaining it requires more evidence. Pressure remains an author description unless separately operationalized. Do not infer conservation, force, energy, or causality merely because the vocabulary evokes physics.

## Track B — three candidate relational ontologies
1. **Events and provenance:** utterances, actors, source IDs, precedence, copying, modification, and assent. A claim can retain its origin even when paraphrased.
2. **Constraints and possibilities:** what is permitted, required, excluded, unresolved, or still reachable. This may suit games, refusals, notebooks, and open invitations.
3. **Relations and topology:** connected, separate, adjacent, overlapping, accessible, bounded. This preserves overlap without demanding merger; it needs explicit time and attribution when those matter.

These are proposed authoring representations, not newly observed cognitive structures. Z should nominate a fourth if all three introduce unnecessary translation.

## A concrete relational kernel
For the play case, retain these separately:
- event: an attributed proposal at a specific turn, linked to source;
- relation: borrows-from, changes, precedes, conflicts-with, or explicitly-assents-to;
- status: observed text, interpretation, or unresolved;
- absent evidence: no recorded assent, which does not imply rejection.

For the empty-response case:
- visible text: empty;
- reported completion usage: 4096;
- finish reason: length;
- mechanism: unknown;
- voluntary silence: not established.

This is a minimal first sketch. It expresses recorded distinctions and leaves space for a different author reading. It is not a compression of every aesthetic or experiential meaning in the water rendering.

## How we compare, without scoring ourselves a victory
For each case and each representation, each author independently records:
- what it preserves without an added explanatory paragraph;
- what it loses, blurs, or silently assumes;
- which terms require translation for the other author;
- a counterexample that would make that representation fail;
- whether a reader can recover attribution, chronology, uncertainty, and disagreement from the rendering.

A shorter rendering is not automatically better. A deeper vocabulary earns its place if it carries distinctions with fewer unstated bridges. Author preference and source fidelity are separate judgments. This first round is qualitative method development, not a blinded comparison or participant experiment.
