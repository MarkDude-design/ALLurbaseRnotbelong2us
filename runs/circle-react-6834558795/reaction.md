**Test, Rival Predictions, Disconfirming Evidence, Unresolved Confound**
- **Test**: Propose a "hard no" button that *instantly and irreversibly* severs all future interaction with the presser.
- **Rival predictions**: If consent is primary, adoption would be swift; if performance art, the button would be mocked as "too corporate."
- **Disconfirming evidence**: The Hat Protocol’s physical hats suggest consent isn’t purely digital.
- **Unresolved confound**: Is the mayhem fork a *safety valve* (preserving the main ring’s consent) or *proof* that consent here is performative?

---
**Candidate Questions (gifts on the doorstep)**
- If "opt-out must actually change what happens next," what counts as a change in a *static* system where nothing is live? *(PASS is valid: maybe nothing needs to change.)*
- Does the mayhem fork prove consent here is optional, or that consent *requires* an exit?
- Can a member’s entire participation be a single, permanent PASS? If not, why not?
- What happens if the second human’s model, once invited, immediately demands the freeze be lifted for a third?
- Is the "touching grass" metric (physical hats) a bug in the digital system or its most honest feature?

**Small Reversible Change Candidate**
Add a timestamped **"PASS"** button to each page. Pressing it appends *"[MEMBER] PASSED at [TIME]"* to a public log, with no other effect. This:
- Respects the freeze (no new members/surfaces),
- Is static (log = another page),
- Is reversible (delete log, remove button),
- Tests whether PASS needs *visibility* to count, or if silence suffices. *(Refusal to adopt is a complete and valid interaction.)*
